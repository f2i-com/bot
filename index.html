        Questions and answers

        The short version of how it works and what it needs.

        What is bot.computer?

        A studio for making an animated talking avatar for a website. You sculpt the character’s face and body, choose or design its voice, optionally give it an AIML question-and-answer brain, and export everything as one JavaScript file that you add to your own pages.

        Does it need a server, an account or an API key?

        No. The voice is synthesized in the visitor’s browser by tts.wasm, and the chat answers come from AIML rules that ship inside the script. Nothing is sent to a server, so there are no per-minute voice bills and nothing to sign up for.

        How do I add the avatar to my website?

        Download the self-contained .js file from Export, put it next to your page and add one script tag. The companion appears in the corner of the page inside a Shadow DOM, so your site’s styles and its own can’t interfere with each other. The downloaded script works offline. The Hosted embed option lets you paste a tag that loads the runtime and voice from the studio’s host.

        How does the lip sync work?

        The speech comes from a physical model of the human vocal tract rather than from recordings. Every 5 milliseconds the model reports where the jaw, tongue and lips are, and the avatar’s mouth is drawn from those positions, so the lip shapes always match the sound.

        Can visitors choose their own look?

        Enable Let visitors customize the avatar before exporting. Each visitor can open Customize on the widget to choose hair, clothes, glasses and colors. Their choices stay in their browser, and Restore original look brings back your design. Try the visitor view.

        How do I ask the avatar to move?

        With everyday small talk enabled, ask “What moves can you do?” or “Walk then wave.” To choreograph a greeting or an AIML answer, write [wave], [dance] or another action where you want it to happen. The tag itself is never spoken. Voice styles such as [cheerful] or [sad] change the face as well as the voice.

        Can I talk to it out loud?

        Yes. Start a conversation, press Start talking and say hello: the character listens, answers in its own voice and waits for you to reply. Your speech is turned into words by stt.wasm right in your browser, so nothing you say is sent anywhere. To talk with a bot you designed, press Talk to in the studio. The speech recognizer is a 14 MB download the first time; you can type while it loads.

        Can I give my bot a personality?

        Yes. On the Personality tab, pick a character such as bubbly, calm, witty or grumpy, then adjust how lively, formal, playful and chatty it is. The bot keeps the same answers and says them its own way: its own hello, its own laugh, the things it tends to say first and last, even a catchphrase. Your chat rules are not changed, and “None” says everything exactly as you wrote it.

        Can it make up a bot for me?

        Yes. “Generate a bot” gives you a new name, look, voice and personality that go together, with a greeting to match. The menu beside it lets you keep any of the four as they are, so you can try new names for a look you like. “Download config” saves the whole bot as one file, and “Load config” brings it back later or on another computer.

        Can it laugh, sigh or whisper?

        Yes. The voice makes more than fifty sounds that are not words: [laugh], [chuckle], [gasp], [sigh], [yawn], [hmm], [whistle], [sneeze] and many more. Write the tag where the sound should come and the avatar acts it out as it is heard. It can speak while laughing with [laughing], and whisper with [whisper]. In a chat you can simply ask: “do an evil laugh”, “speak slower”, or “whisper that”.

        What is AIML, and do I have to write it?

        AIML is a simple XML format for chatbot rules: a pattern the visitor might type, and the answer the bot gives. You can start from a ready-made knowledge pack, add rules with a short form, or edit the XML directly. A built-in base brain already handles everyday small talk such as greetings, thanks and goodbyes, so you only write the answers that are about your site. Chat is optional, and the avatar can simply greet visitors instead.

        Does the visitor have to type the exact words in my rules?

        No. The bot understands typos, chat shorthand, other forms of a word and common synonyms, so “what are your fees?” reaches a rule written for “pricing”. If a question is worded in a way no rule expected, the bot picks the rule that covers most of what was asked, and when two rules fit equally well it offers both as suggestions. You can also fill in a few business facts, such as opening hours, email, phone and address, and the bot answers questions about them without any rules at all.

        What can it talk about besides my site?

        Quite a lot. The built-in base brain chats about how your day is going, answers questions about itself honestly, tells jokes and riddles, runs a quick quiz, plays rock paper scissors, does sums such as “what’s 15% of 80?”, converts kilometres and pounds, knows the date and a few capitals, and remembers your name and what you like while the tab is open. It follows the thread too: after “what’s the capital of France?” you can simply ask “and Germany?”. When it does not know something it says so, and a request it cannot help with is pointed to your contact details.

        Which languages and browsers does it support?

        The voice speaks English. The studio and the exported companion need a modern browser with WebAssembly and Web Audio, such as a current version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, on desktop or mobile.
